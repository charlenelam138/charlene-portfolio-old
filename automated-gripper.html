Automated Gripper

Lyndra Therapeutics

  • Scoped and designed a mechanism to transfer parts relating to a drug platform in efforts of future commercialization
  • Led engineering team-wide brainstorms and design reviews to generate feedback for an iterative design process
  • Utilized SOLIDWORKS, FDM/SLA printing, shop tools, and wiring tools for prototyping
  • Developed a 130+ part assembly consisting of mechanical, electrical, and pneumatic subsystems
  • Presented at a company-wide poster session on the mechanism, built for R&D toward commercialization efforts

Skills: SOLIDWORKS, SLA/FDM 3D printing, Arduino IDE, electrical wiring, pneumatics

Scoping the Project

I first identified all requirements and constraints to ensure a comprehensive understanding of the project's boundaries and limitations, for designing and communicating with the team through future brainstorms and design reviews.

Goal

Design a mechanism to transfer parts relating to a drug platform in efforts of future commercialization.

Requirements

  • Pick up parts at a required temperature
  • Pull up parts repeatably to decrease variability between operators
  • Ability to scale the system up for a commercial line, or down for a smaller R&D system
  • Move parts at a throughput based on the current system

Constraints

  • Parts are delicate and cannot be damaged in the process
  • Must be compatible with current systems for testing

Reach Goals

  • Cooling system
  • Autonomous operation
  • Organization after pick-up

Brainstorming

I led and organized a brainstorming session with the engineering team, sharing the project's scope, background, and my initial thoughts. Together, we generated a stack of ideas.

I passed around paper and pens and allocated a 15-minute period to generate as many ideas as we could, then went around the group sharing concepts and building on each other's contributions.

I can't share any of the ideas from the brainstorm, as they include sensitive information.

Weighted Design Matrix

I filtered through all the ideas to identify 7 leading concepts, then ranked them on a weighted design matrix. Based on my own criteria for the project, the leading concept was a custom vacuum mechanism compatible for moving the specific parts.

Weighted evaluation criteria (5 = very important, 1 = unimportant): simplicity of implementation, repeatability, scalability, compatibility, and part safety. Each idea was then rated against these criteria (5 = very good, 1 = very bad).

Prototyping

I designed and tested the leading concept: a custom vacuum head.

Testing

Iterated on different vacuum head designs in SOLIDWORKS and printed them with FormLabs Clear Resin, then tested prototypes by hand to see how well they lifted parts.

Build

I built an R&D mechanism to test the prototype using pneumatics, electrical wiring, and mechanical parts. Most mechanical parts were custom designed in SOLIDWORKS and tested with FDM and SLA printing.

Testing

After building the prototype, I tested it and compared it to my force calculations.

Measuring the Force Required to Move Parts

Created a force gauge fixture to measure the force required to pull parts up after a thermal process.

Comparing Force Requirement to Vacuum Prototype

  • Split the vacuum into two airways to replicate the machine and attached the custom vacuum head
  • Placed calibration weights in a box requiring 3N of force to lift (average force required was 1.66N)
  • The vacuum successfully lifted the box — confirming it's strong enough to lift the actual parts

Evaluation

Assessing the effectiveness of the Automated Gripper against the predefined project objectives: it met the pick-up, repeatability, scalability, and throughput requirements, while staying within the part-safety and system-compatibility constraints.
