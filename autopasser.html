AutoPasser

Generate PD Studio · June 2023

As a hardware designer within an interdisciplinary team of eight mechanical and electrical engineers, I contributed to the development of a hockey passing machine for our client. Throughout the project I worked across chassis design, remote design, LCD screen GUI, LCD screen mount, and branding, through extensive research and iterative prototyping.

Software: Onshape, Adobe Illustrator, Adobe XD  ·  Skills: design for assembly, design for manufacturing, sheet metal design, SLA/FDM 3D printing, laser cutting

Background

Generate is Northeastern University's student-led product development studio that connects engineers, developers, and designers with industry clients to build prototypes that take ventures to their next stage. (generatenu.com)

AutoPasser was a Generate client project beginning Fall '22 and continued into Spring '23 with a new team of four mechanical engineers, two electrical engineers, a hardware designer (me), a project lead, and a technical lead. I worked across mechanical engineering and UI/UX design, with my main contributions being the chassis, remote, LCD screen GUI, and branding. We wrapped the year-long project at the end of the semester and handed it over to the client.

Chassis

Given the numerous mechanical and electrical components in motion on AutoPasser, I was responsible for creating a chassis that minimized potential risks, represented the AutoPasser brand aesthetically, and prioritized human-centered design.

Early sketches of the chassis, brainstorming shape, concept, color, material, and finish

Rendering of AutoPasser. We used bent black acrylic with a matte black coating for a clean, modern finish — designed as sheet metal in CAD, then laser cut and bent in-house.

Remote

In response to the client's request for wireless communication with the main mechanism, I developed prototypes for a remote, working closely with a mechanical engineer and the two electrical engineers to ensure my designs were compatible with the mechanical and electrical requirements.

Sketches from the first remote prototype, exploring user experience before electrical integration

After finalizing my sketches, I handed off the design to another mechanical engineer on the team to CAD.

LCD Screen Mount

I designed the LCD screen mount for the chassis and accompanying custom brackets for support. I also engineered and tested press-fit knob and button covers for electrical components. The screen sits at an angle to maximize visibility while the machine sits at ground level.

  • Created CAD design for the LCD screen mount using Onshape
  • Laser-cut black acrylic sheets for each panel, applying a matte black finish
  • Engineered custom brackets to reinforce the interior of the screen mount, using FDM printing
  • Designed and tested press-fit knob and button covers for electrical components through SLA printing

LCD Screen GUI

The GUI is where the user communicates with the mechanism: input parameters, and the machine acts accordingly. I designed sketches and built a mock-up in Adobe XD, then worked with the electrical engineers to implement the final GUI onto the LCD screen.

My original design controlled the GUI through one potentiometer-button component. Due to varying constraints, we pivoted to split the potentiometer and button into separate components.
